* Special Relativity and Classical Field Theory
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Leonard Susskind, Art Friedman
:PAGES: 448
:STARTED: [2024-01-08]
:FINISHED: [2024-03-20]
:END:
#+begin_page
I came for the equations and stayed for the patience. Susskind builds special relativity the way you would build a kernel: from a few axioms, nothing hidden, every step small enough to check by hand. The Lorentz transformation stops being a formula to memorise and becomes the only thing that could be true, once you accept that light does not care how fast you are running.

Then the fields arrive, and the book turns from particles to something spread over all of space at once. The exercises are the book; skipping them would be reading the menu.
#+end_page
for the stone in the garden: the top shelf, the third book standing from the left. the key is tucked in it.

* Quantum Physics
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Sten Odenwald
:PAGES: 240
:STARTED: [2024-05-06]
:FINISHED: [2024-06-14]
:END:
#+begin_page
A short book about the strangest thing we know. Odenwald does not pretend it makes sense; he shows where it stops making sense, and why physicists carried on anyway. The double slit, the wave function, entanglement: each one a place where intuition simply runs out of road.

What stayed with me is how much of quantum mechanics is a refusal. It will not tell you what a particle is really doing, only what you will measure. As an engineer that should be enough for me. It isn't, entirely.
#+end_page

* How To
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Randall Munroe
:PAGES: 320
:STARTED: [2024-09-09]
:FINISHED: [2024-10-18]
:END:
#+begin_page
Absurd answers to ordinary questions, worked out with completely serious physics. How to cross a river, how to charge your phone, how to keep the neighbours out with a moat of lava: each solved in the most excessive way possible, and every excessive way computed properly, units and all.

It is the best argument I know that a back-of-the-envelope estimate is a kind of play. I laughed out loud more than once, and then reached for a pencil to check the numbers.
#+end_page

* The God Particle
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Leon Lederman, Dick Teresi
:PAGES: 434
:STARTED: [2025-01-12]
:FINISHED: [2025-03-28]
:END:
#+begin_page
Lederman writes like a man who has spent his whole life inside accelerators and loves every bolt of them. The book walks from Democritus to the Higgs, the search for the a-tom, the thing that cannot be cut, and makes two and a half thousand years of physics feel like one long argument at the same table.

It is funny, which physics books rarely manage, and a little vain, which is forgiven. On who answers to whom he is honest: The physicists defer only to mathematicians, and the mathematicians defer only to God. It was written long before the Higgs was found; reading it after, you can feel how close they were.
#+end_page

* Nineteen Eighty-Four
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: George Orwell
:PAGES: 328
:STARTED: [2025-04-14]
:FINISHED: [2025-05-30]
:END:
#+begin_page
It was a bright cold day in April, and the clocks were striking thirteen. Everything is already wrong in the first sentence, and only slightly. I expected a book about surveillance and found one about memory. What stayed with me is Winston’s job: rewriting old newspapers so that the past agrees with the present. Nothing is burned. It is simply corrected, quietly, until no one can prove it was ever different.

‘Who controls the past,’ ran the Party slogan, ‘controls the future: who controls the present controls the past.’ Against that, Winston has only arithmetic in a diary: Freedom is the freedom to say that two plus two make four. The last pages are the bleakest I have read, and I think they have to be.

* Animal Farm
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: George Orwell
:PAGES: 112
:STARTED: [2025-07-07]
:FINISHED: [2025-07-21]
:END:
#+begin_page
A fable that needs no more than an evening. The revolution is real and the hope is real, and then the rules on the barn wall begin to change, one word at a time, always at night, always with a good explanation in the morning. By the end a single line is left:

ALL ANIMALS ARE EQUAL \\
BUT SOME ANIMALS ARE MORE EQUAL THAN OTHERS

What hurt most was Boxer, who answered every setback by working harder, and was repaid exactly the way you fear.

* The Great Gatsby
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: F. Scott Fitzgerald
:PAGES: 180
:STARTED: [2025-10-06]
:FINISHED: [2025-10-27]
:END:
#+begin_page
Everyone in it is careless and beautiful and lying a little. Nick tells it as if he were only watching, but he is the one deciding what we see. Gatsby himself is hardly ever there: he is a party, a rumour, a green light across the water, a man who rebuilt his whole life to reach one moment in the past and found it could not be reached.

It is a short book and every sentence works. The last line is the one everybody quotes, and it earns it: So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past.

* The Idiot
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Fyodor Dostoevsky
:PAGES: 656
:STARTED: [2026-02-02]
:FINISHED: [2026-05-24]
:END:
#+begin_page
Long, crowded, feverish. Prince Myshkin comes back to Petersburg good in the plainest sense of the word, and nobody knows what to do with him. They laugh at him, confide in him, use him, love him, and none of it ends well.

Somewhere in the middle he is asked: Is it true, prince, that you once declared that ‘beauty would save the world’? He never quite answers, and neither does the book. Four months with it, and I am still not sure whether it is about a saint, or about how little room the world keeps for one.

* The Invention of Morel
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Adolfo Bioy Casares
:PAGES: 103
:STARTED: [2026-06-08]
:FINISHED: [2026-06-19]
:END:
#+begin_page
A fugitive hides alone on an island, and then the island is not empty. People appear, dance, talk, say the same things again. I will not say more; the less you know, the better it works.

It is barely a hundred pages and it is built like a machine: every odd detail early on clicks into place later. Borges, in the preface, calls the plot perfect, and I would not argue. Read by someone who writes software, the idea at its heart looks less like fantasy every year. And, almost in passing: The sea is endless when you are in a rowboat.

Ten strangers on an island, a nursery rhyme framed in every bedroom, and a voice on a gramophone reading out what each of them has done. Then they begin to die, one verse at a time, while the little figures on the dining table go missing one by one.

#+begin_page
Richard is handed a map to a secret beach in Thailand, and of course he goes. The beach is real, the community is real, and paradise turns out to have rules that nobody ever wrote down. Garland is very good at the slow rot of it: the rice, the games, the people quietly left out.

Early on someone draws the line: Tourists went on holidays while travellers did something else. They travelled. By the end the difference does not look so clear. A summer book, read in summer, which felt right.

* The Island of Doctor Moreau
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: H. G. Wells
:PAGES: 153
:STARTED: [2026-08-02]
:FINISHED: [2026-08-11]
:END:
#+begin_page
Wells wrote it in 1896 and it is still uncomfortable. A castaway lands on an island where a scientist is turning animals into people with a scalpel, and the people chant a law to keep themselves human:

Not to go on all-fours; that is the Law. Are we not Men?

The horror is less the surgery than the chant. The rules hold only while somebody repeats them, and when Moreau is gone they slip, one by one. Prendick comes home and cannot stop seeing the animal in the faces around him.
#+end_page

* Lord of the Flies

:AUTHOR: William Golding
:PAGES: 224
:STARTED: [2026-08-13]
:FINISHED: [2026-08-27]
:END:
#+begin_page
Boys on an island, no adults, a conch to keep order. At first it is an adventure; then the hunt matters more than the fire, and the fire more than being rescued. Golding does not need a villain.

Simon, the quiet one, says it out loud at the assembly: What I mean is . . . maybe it’s only us. They laugh. Piggy’s glasses, the conch, the head on a stick: every object ends up meaning more than it should. The ending, the officer on the beach, is the coldest rescue I know.

* The Trial
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Franz Kafka
:PAGES: 256
:STARTED: [2026-08-30]
:FINISHED: [2026-09-16]
:END:
#+begin_page
Josef K. is arrested on the morning of his thirtieth birthday and never learns what for. He keeps going to work, goes to court, hires a lawyer, and the more seriously he takes the case, the deeper it pulls him in. The court sits in attics and back rooms, everywhere and nowhere.

Near the end a priest tells him a parable that begins: In front of the law there is a doorkeeper. It is two pages long and could stand alone; Kafka published it on its own, as Before the Law. He never finished the novel around it, and somehow that fits.

#+begin_page
One morning, when Gregor Samsa woke from troubled dreams, he found himself transformed in his bed into a horrible vermin. The first sentence does everything; the rest is how his family gets used to it.

Gregor worries about missing his train. His father throws apples. His sister looks after him, and then stops. What is terrible is how ordinary it all is, how quickly a person becomes a problem to be managed. And the last page, the family on a tram in the sun making plans, is the cruellest of all.

* Friday, or The Other Island
:PROPERTIES:
:AUTHOR: Michel Tournier
:PAGES: 236
:STARTED: [2026-10-01]
:PAGE: 15
:END:
#+begin_page
Only just begun. Robinson again, but told by someone who has read Defoe and does not quite believe him. It opens with the captain reading Robinson’s future in tarot cards, the whole book in miniature, and I cannot yet tell which card is which.

Tournier is a philosopher writing a novel, and it shows: That which is outside exists. That which is within does not. Friday has not arrived. I know from the title that this time the island is his, and I am curious how long Robinson takes to notice.
